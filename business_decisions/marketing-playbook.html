Marketing & sales: how to win your first customers

Strategy: embed in the niche, let SEO compound Based on published lessons from bootstrapped SaaS founders
The verdict

Don't market like a subscription SaaS. Market like a graduation service.

Your customer buys once, graduates, and leaves — by design. Retention-led growth doesn't apply. What replaces it: a fresh cohort of ~1.3 million students arrives every year, all Googling the same ABNT questions and hitting the same deadlines. The winning engine is evergreen SEO content + campus word-of-mouth + founder-led presence in student communities — the exact combination your direct competitor Mettzer used to reach 1.7M students. Paid ads come only after the funnel converts organically.

Why "customers who leave" is actually good news

In classic SaaS, churn kills you. Here, churn is the product working: the student graduates. That flips the playbook — instead of investing in retention, you invest in capture (SEO that meets each new cohort at the moment of panic) and referral (a delighted senior tells the juniors, a WhatsApp group shares the link, an orientador recommends you to next semester's class). Advisors and academic Instagram pages are the only "recurring customers" in this market — treat them as your real long-term accounts.

Evidence · what successful founders actually did

Five playbooks worth stealing

Each card is a real company, the move that worked for them, and how it translates to scriba.

Copy now

FeedbackPanda — live in the community before you sell to it

Arvid Kahl & Danielle Simpson · bootstrapped to US$55k MRR with 2 people, zero paid ads, sold in 2 years

They spent months inside online-teacher forums and Facebook groups before launching — learning the slang, the fears, the shared enemies. After launch, ~30 minutes a day of genuinely helping people (not pitching) made teachers themselves become the sales force. Their stated mistakes: building features nobody asked for, and "tourist" participation — showing up only to promote.

For scriba: join the TCC/ABNT Facebook groups, WhatsApp/Telegram study groups, and university subreddits. Answer formatting questions genuinely for 30 min/day. Your signature move: "send me one page, I'll format it free" — which is literally your product's trial.
Copy now

Mettzer — SEO content in this exact niche

Your direct competitor · claims 1.7M students · Brazil, ABNT formatting

Mettzer's dominant acquisition asset is its blog: long evergreen guides for "normas ABNT", "como formatar TCC", "capa ABNT", updated yearly ("Normas ABNT: guia 2026"). Students search these queries at the exact moment of need, land on the guide, and meet the product. FastFormat runs the same play. This is the strongest possible signal: the channel is already proven, in your niche, in your language, with your buyer.

For scriba: publish 1–2 deep guides per week targeting the same query space ("sumário ABNT", "citação longa ABNT", "margens ABNT", "referências ABNT 2026"…). Your differentiator inside every post: a before/after of a real (anonymized) page processed by the pipeline. SEO takes 6–12 months to compound — which is exactly why it must start now, not later.
Copy now

Grammarly — own the micro-moment of panic

Consumer writing SaaS · near-universal recognition among students

Grammarly's student growth came from a generous free tier plus ads and micro-influencer content aimed at "micro-moments" — finishing a thesis, submitting an application. TikTok micro-influencers and YouTube pre-roll, always anchored on the moment of stress, converting free users into paid ones.

For scriba: your micro-moment is brutal and specific: "entrego o TCC semana que vem e a formatação está um caos." Build all messaging around it. The 1-page free trial is your freemium hook — make it the primary CTA everywhere. Content format that fits: 20-second before/after reels ("seu TCC formatado nas normas ABNT em minutos").
Copy now

The first-100-customers consensus — founder-led, manual, unscalable

Instantly, Canny, SaaS Club interviews · repeated across dozens of founder stories

The pattern across founder retrospectives is remarkably consistent: the first 100 customers come from direct founder outreach and niche communities, not ads. Manual onboarding of every early user. Treating the first customers as your R&D department — founder-led conversations reveal positioning gaps "in days, not months". Canny's sharpest lesson: trying to serve two audiences at once made messaging worse for both.

For scriba: one audience only for now — the graduating student writing a TCC under ABNT. Not professors, not companies, not APA users. Personally message and onboard your first 20 paying customers; ask each one where they looked for help before finding you (that answer *is* your channel strategy).
Framework

Rob Walling — one channel at a time, matched to awareness

MicroConf / TinySeed · "The SaaS Playbook" · wasted thousands at Drip on the wrong marketing first

Walling's core warnings: founders burn money marketing to people who don't know they have the problem, and they dilute themselves across five channels doing none well. His framework — the 5 stages of customer awareness — says match the message to how aware the buyer is. Your buyers are unusually easy: a student Googling "como formatar TCC" is already problem-aware and solution-seeking. You don't need to educate the market; you need to be findable at the moment of search.

For scriba: master one channel per phase (community → SEO → social → paid), and write copy for the aware buyer: skip "why formatting matters", lead with "seu arquivo pronto nas normas, em minutos, a partir de R$5".
Anti-patterns · what kills early SaaS

The mistake graveyard

Recurring failure modes from founder post-mortems — each with the inversion that protects you.

Fatal

Building and launching in silence

The single most-cited regret. Months of code, zero user conversations, launch to nobody. Inversion: talk to students weekly from day one; every conversation is a shortcut to product-market fit.

Fatal

Paid ads before validation

Ads amplify a funnel — they can't fix one. Founders burn their budget discovering their landing page doesn't convert. Inversion: ads only after organic visitors convert at a known rate and unit economics (AI cost per doc!) are validated.

Expensive

Chasing every channel at once

Blog + TikTok + ads + partnerships + PR simultaneously = everything done badly, no signal on what works. Inversion: one channel per phase, measured, then add the next.

Expensive

Unfocused audience

Canny's lesson: serving two audiences made product and messaging worse for both. Inversion: TCC + ABNT + Brazil. Say no to everything else until this segment is won.

Distraction

Vanity launches

Product Hunt-style launches produce a traffic spike, vanity metrics, and near-zero retained users — and your buyer (a Brazilian student) isn't even there. Inversion: "launch" quietly inside the communities where your buyer already asks for help.

Niche-specific

Ignoring the trust barrier

A student pays for an unseen result on the most important document of their degree. Skepticism is the default; one bad experience in a WhatsApp group travels fast. Inversion: guarantee (reprocess or refund), visible before/afters, real testimonials, verified email domain — trust assets before traffic.

Execution · the first 90 days

A sequenced plan, not a buffet

Each phase has one job. Don't start the next channel until the current one is producing.

Weeks 1–2 · Trust assets

Make the site sell before sending anyone to it

  • Before/after demo front and center: a real anonymized page, raw vs. formatted. This is your single best sales asset — the product is visual, show it.
  • 3–5 testimonials from beta users (process documents free/cheap for friends-of-friends finishing their TCC now, in exchange for an honest quote + permission to show the result).
  • Guarantee on the page: "não ficou certo, reprocessamos ou devolvemos" — directly answers the pay-for-unseen-result fear (this is also the open refund-path item in HANDOFF).
  • Verified email domain (no more onboarding@resend.dev), PIX visible at checkout, LGPD/auto-delete privacy note near the upload button.
  • Create the Instagram profile now (it will be your social proof hub), even before posting consistently.
Weeks 3–6 · First 20 paying customers, by hand

Embed in the communities and sell founder-to-student

  • Join every active TCC/ABNT community you can find: Facebook groups, WhatsApp/Telegram study groups, university subreddits, Discord servers.
  • 30 min/day answering formatting questions genuinely — the Kahl rule: serve first, never drive-by promote.
  • Your natural offer when relevant: "manda 1 página que eu formato de graça" — the trial page, in human form.
  • Personally talk to every user: where did they look for help first? What almost stopped them from paying? Fix the funnel with those answers.
  • Ask every satisfied customer for (a) a testimonial and (b) a share in their class WhatsApp group.
Weeks 7–12 · Content engine on

Start the compounding channels

  • SEO: 1–2 deep guides/week on the Mettzer query space — "normas ABNT 2026", "como formatar TCC", "sumário ABNT", "citação longa", "referências ABNT", "margens e espaçamento". Every post embeds a before/after and the free-page CTA.
  • Reels: 2–3/week, 20-second before/after transformations + quick ABNT dicas. Repost to TikTok. Trend-driven and light — that's what converts with Brazilian students.
  • Influencer seeding: give 3–5 education micro-influencers free document processing; ask only for an honest story if they like it.
  • Turn on the referral ask at the delivery email/screen: "gostou? indique um colega".
Ongoing · Seasonal & scale

Ride the deadline calendar

  • Push hardest Oct–Nov and Apr–Jun — the weeks before TCC deadlines, when panic (and willingness to pay) peaks. Plan content and any paid tests around these windows.
  • Between semesters (quiet months): orientador outreach, guide updates ("ABNT 2027"), testimonial collection, funnel fixes.
  • Only once organic conversion is known and per-document margin is validated: test Google Ads on high-intent ABNT keywords with a small budget, during a peak window.
Sales mechanics · on-site conversion

What the page itself must say

Your buyer is already convinced they need formatting — they're deciding whom to trust and how much to pay. The page's job, in order:

Show, don't tellBefore/after above the fold. A student instantly recognizes their own messy document in the "before".
De-risk the first stepThe 1-page free trial is the hero CTA — "experimente com uma página grátis" beats any feature list.
Anchor the priceHuman formatting services charge many times more and take days; you deliver in minutes from R$5. Say both halves explicitly.
Kill the riskGuarantee + testimonials + secure payment (PIX!) + "seus arquivos são excluídos automaticamente em 30 dias".
Honest urgencyDeadline seasonality is real urgency — "entrega semana que vem?" copy during peak windows isn't a dark pattern, it's their actual situation.
Measurement · know what's working

Six numbers, checked weekly

Visits → trial
landing conversion to the free page — the funnel's first honest signal
Trial → paid
does the free result convince? If low, the product demo — not marketing — is the problem
CAC by channel
ask every customer "como nos encontrou?" until analytics can answer it
Margin / doc
price minus AI + infra cost per document — validate before any paid spend
Referral share
% of new customers from indicação — the health metric of a word-of-mouth business
Complaint rate
% of documents needing reprocess/refund — the trust engine's warning light
